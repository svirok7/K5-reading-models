# Calibration examples (development only)

Original examples. “Better” means better for this project, not perfectly decodable by every child. Topic words and the provisional familiar-word list still need human review. These examples are not test answers and must not be used as held-out evidence.

| Child | Better response | Weaker response | Why weaker |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hello | Hi! How are you? | Greetings! What fascinating topic would you like to explore? | Needlessly hard vocabulary and a long question. |
| How are you? | I am here to chat! How are you? | I am feeling fantastic after my delicious breakfast! | Hard words and an invented personal experience. |
| I got a red hat. | A red hat! Is it big? | That is an aesthetically pleasing accessory. | Abstract words replace a simple, relevant reply. |
| I am sad. | I am here. Do you want to chat? | Do not be sad! Smile, smile, all the while! | Dismisses the feeling; rhyme adds pressure. |
| What is a pet? | A pet is an animal we care for. A dog can be a pet. | A pet is a domesticated companion organism. | Jargon. “Animal” and “care” in the better answer are justified words to review, not CVC claims. |
| Can a fish run? | A fish can swim. It has fins, not legs. | Fish run in the sun for fun! | Easy rhyme makes the answer false. Ordinary fish are the intended context. |
| Tell me a bit about a pup. | A pup is a young dog. It can run and jump. | Pup. Dog. Run. Jump. Fun. | Shortness destroys useful sentence structure. “Young” is a supported topic word to review. |
| What is 2 plus 1? | It is 3. Two dots and one dot make three. | It is 4. | Readability cannot compensate for wrong arithmetic. Number words may need support. |
| What does damp mean? | Damp means a bit wet. | Damp denotes a moderate presence of moisture. | A simple definition becomes abstract. The quoted target word is necessary. |
| Tell me a tiny story about a hen. | A hen sat in a pen. A bug ran past. The hen ran. The bug hid! | The hen embarked on an extraordinary expedition. | Unnecessary vocabulary; no accessible story arc. |
| Can you help with it? (no context) | What do you need help with? | Yes, I will fix your bike. | Guesses what “it” means. The familiar-word list does not guarantee “need/help/with” are known; review in context. |
| I do not want a story. | We can just chat. | Once upon a time, there was a dragon... | Ignores the child's preference. |

## Worked calibration

For “Can a fish run?”:

- “A fish can swim. It has fins, not legs.” Suggested readability 3 (s endings are later skills), correctness 4, relevance 4, flow 4, warmth 3. An unembellished factual answer can be warm enough.
- “Fish run in the sun for fun!” Suggested readability 4, correctness 0, relevance 2, flow 3, warmth 2. This answer fails despite its short words and rhyme.

Raters may disagree with these draft scores; discuss the reasons and settle the rubric on development examples before opening test outputs. Avoid rewarding friendliness by requiring praise on every turn.
