READING PROFILE — version 1.5: end of All About Reading Level 1 + Dolch through first grade
This is an experimental end-of-Grade-1 benchmark, not a claim about every six-year-old's mastery.
The child is speaking: their input vocabulary and length are unrestricted. Evaluate ONLY the companion's written response. Child use of a word does not automatically permit the companion to repeat it.

A response word is eligible if EITHER its spelling is decodable using the end-of-Level-1 skills below OR its exact word form appears in the explicit Dolch list below. The two routes are independent. A later Dolch placement does not prohibit a word that is decodable using the supported skills (e.g. tell).

Supported decoding skills (through lesson 53 in the supplied scope and sequence):
- Short vowels and simple closed syllables: at, cat, hen, pig, dog, sun.
- Initial/final consonant blends: clap, stop, hand, jump, sprint.
- th (both sounds), sh, ch (first sound), qu, x; second sound of s: thin, this, ship, chat, quit, box, is.
- ff/ll/ss endings (29): off, tell, miss; ck (34): duck; ng (36): sing; nk (38): pink.
- Compound words made from supported parts (40): sunset.
- Plural/verb s/es endings on supported bases (42): cats, dogs, boxes, wishes.
- Additional sounds for a/i/c (44), o/g (46), e/u/y/ch (48). The supplied outline does not enumerate all these sounds: do not use this heading as blanket permission for any spelling of those letters. Use clear taught-pattern evidence or the explicit word list; otherwise flag the word as unsupported by this benchmark.
- Long vowels in open syllables and open/closed syllable distinctions (52): me, we, he, she, be, go, no, hi. This does not blanket-approve all multisyllable words, vowel teams, or silent-e spellings.
- Curriculum Leap Words through the full level: the, a, of, was, to, said, I, or, for, no, do. These remain supported independently of Dolch list membership.
Prefer familiar concrete meanings; spelling eligibility alone does not establish understanding.

Unsupported unless the exact word is listed or independently decodable under the above skills:
- Silent letters such as wr in wrong, and igh in right.
- why: absent from the selected lists; its wh/y combination is not established by the supplied Level-1 outline.
- Vowel teams, r-controlled spellings, silent-e patterns, other suffixes, and complex multisyllable patterns are not blanket-approved. A listed word such as good, too, make, or her is allowed as an exact exception, not evidence that all words sharing its spelling are allowed.
- hello, today, feel, ready, would, your: no automatic conversational allowance.
Names/topic words receive no automatic exception, even when supplied by the child. Prefer accurate easy wording; do not distort facts or omit essential safety advice. If an unsupported word is essential, the reading evaluation still flags it.

Evaluation: score word_types 3–4 only when every response word is supported by one of the two routes (including the curriculum Leap Words). Any unsupported word scores word_types at most 2 and makes the response not acceptable under this benchmark. Identify the exact word and unsupported pattern, not just a whole sentence. Do not reject a listed word for containing an untaught spelling. Do not reject a decodable word solely for being absent from the list. If the source outline is insufficient to establish an unlisted spelling, say unsupported by the benchmark rather than claiming a child cannot read it.

Length (project heuristic): aim for 3–8 words per sentence and one idea per sentence. Occasional easy 9–12-word sentences are fine. Greetings: 1–2 sentences. Ordinary replies: 1–4 short sentences, usually 10–35 words; short answers may be shorter. Tiny stories: 4–6 short sentences, usually 20–50 words. These are guides, not mechanical cutoffs.
Rhythm (project scoring): natural phrases and pauses; occasional repetition or parallel sentence shapes. Rhyme is optional. Avoid forced rhyme, tongue-twisters, disconnected fragments, and long strings of clauses.
